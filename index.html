To stop losing track of 100 AI sessions, I built a board.

01 · Seeing every session at once

Session Board

One board showing which of my hundred-plus AI sessions are alive, waiting or done.

The Session Board is how I supervise my AI work sessions. On 2026-09-24 a sweep found at least 100 still open, some three weeks old, with no way to tell which were alive, waiting on me or finished. A status list updated by hand was wrong on 15 of its 22 rows.

I treated it as an interface problem: how does one person read a hundred states at a glance? Each session opens its own page on one board and draws its work as a journey of gates, from its purpose to its closing report. Gates needing my decision look different, and a waiting session must state its exact question. Every row shows its age, so a stale one looks stale, and a page cannot show 100% until its closing report exists.

It went live on 2026-09-24. Whether sessions keep it current is not yet measured.

02 · Finding why my sessions scattered

Finding the scatter

Finding why my AI system kept losing track of its work, before fixing anything.

Finding the scatter. Before moving my AI system to a new computer on 2026-09-24, I checked what existed only on the old one. 163 commits, eight days of work, had never been pushed. At least 100 work sessions were still open. Branches lived on one disk only, one project had no remote, and a status list updated by hand was wrong on 15 of its 22 rows.

I did not fix it first. A separate agent diagnosed the cause; a different one gets the repair. The finding: nothing in the lifecycle ends anything. Work is created at machine speed and ended at human speed, and nothing counts the gap. My own end-of-session procedure stopped at a local merge, and said in writing that nothing enforced it.

The urgent cleanup was done by hand that night. The first prevention is built: since 25 September a census check flags work that exists only on one disk. The rest are designed, not yet built. The OUROBOROS lesson again.

03 · Checking my own AI systems

OUROBOROS

Commission the research, audit it against what’s running, then check the decision actually shipped.

OUROBOROS is a research, audit and decision process I ran on my own AI agent system. It began with a commissioned report, about 77 sources, on self-improving multi-agent architectures and how to evaluate them.

I did not adopt the report. I audited it line by line against what my system actually ran, kept what fit, and refused the rest with reasons: monthly infrastructure budgets in the thousands, GPU clusters, safety layers sourced to vendor marketing.

Then I checked what shipped. Of six approved changes, one had become enforced code; five were only prose. Now a decision ships with its mechanism, or it is recorded as not done. The system it grew into works the same way: every change it proposes to itself is tested against a fixed set of tasks first, and one that does worse is never adopted. Still in progress, it is heading toward an agent orchestration system.

04 · An AI product for knowing yourself

Five Lights

A self-reflection product that tests whether its own writing is actually about you.

Five Lights is a self-reflection product: a person gives their birth details and a short intake, and receives a long written reading. It is live, with a real checkout.

The frameworks behind it are reflection tools, not science, so the question worth measuring is whether the writing is specific to its reader or would fit anyone. I built two instruments for that. The Ten People Test asks a blind judge to place one sentence between two readers’ chart cards, a two-person placement rate; chance is 50%. The corpus scores 60.0% (95% CI 52.7 to 66.9). An audit on 26 September removed the sentences that repeat a chart fact word for word, and the rest score 55.4% (47.6 to 63.0), an interval that includes chance. Every fix to the test so far has lowered the number. A Forer test, where people rate their own reading blind against someone else’s, is collecting its first 17 clean ratings.

05 · How I keep it honest

Rules, leaks, laws and local models

Rules that get graded, a score I keep lowering, a shelf of cited laws, and models measured on my own Mac.
  • Local modelsOn my own Mac mini (M5 Pro, 64 GB) I tested four image models and two video models. One video model finishes a clip. Two local text models scored 17 of 20 on a twenty-task test, matching one cloud run.
  • RulesMy AI agents follow rules, and the rules get graded too. Every rule logs each time it fires. I measured one: it blocked a third of all turns, and about one block in twenty stood on a wrong fact. So a rule marked wrong five times in 28 days, with no right mark, drops to a warning. One right mark restores it.
  • LeaksMy best number was a leak. I built a test: can a blind judge tell which person a sentence of their personal report was written for? Chance is 50%. The first run scored 72.8%. Two blind judges found the test was leaking its own answer key. Each leak I closed brought the score down, to 62.8% and then 60.0%. A later audit took out the sentences that repeat a chart fact, and the rest score 55.4%, a range that includes chance. Every fix has lowered the number, and I publish the lowest.
  • CodexMy rule: open the field’s book first, and cite the law I apply. On 6 October 2026 the shelf held 31 books and over 510 laws, and each law records its evidence and where it breaks. The laws this page applies: selling 4, 5 and 9; marketing 3 and 11; going-viral 5 and 9; storytelling 2, 6, 8, 9, 14, 15 and 19; law-and-trust 3 and 4; research-method 15. The books are private.

Music and DJ tools

Two tools I needed for my own DJ sets and could not find, so I built them.

01 · Visuals for my own DJ sets

PulseEngine

Software I’m building to turn live DJ music into real-time visuals, lyrics and projection.

PulseEngine is real-time visual software for live DJ sets, built for my own. It listens to the music as it plays, locks to the tempo, and drives visuals on the beat. It generates images with AI in real time, transcribes lyrics from the vocals onto the screen as they are sung, and projection-maps the result onto physical surfaces. Motion reactivity is still in development.

The detail that shaped it: an early build crashed on launch from a bug in its audio analysis, and a quieter one made the tempo estimate meaningless. Both are fixed, and the rule since is to test with real sound in the real room before a set, never after.

02 · Preparing my own DJ sets

DJ set preparation tool

Prepares a DJ’s sets: finds what a USB stick has that the computer lacks, and turns a tracklist into a rekordbox playlist.

It has no public name yet. I built it after deleting the music from my own laptop: one stick held 1,939 files, and filenames could not tell me which tracks were already home. So it compares the audio. In a test on my own library, across 120 renamed, re-encoded copies, filename matching found 0 and audio matching found 120.

Two parts of set preparation are built and tested in code: building a set, and writing a tracklist into a rekordbox playlist. Neither has been used on a real gig yet. That first run is still to come.
