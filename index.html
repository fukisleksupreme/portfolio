01 · One for knowing myself

Five Lights

A self-reflection product that tests whether its own writing is actually about you.

Five Lights is a self-reflection product: a person gives their birth details and a short intake, and receives a long written reading. It is live, with a real checkout.

The frameworks behind it are reflection tools, not science, so the question worth measuring is whether the writing is specific to its reader or would fit anyone. I built two instruments for that. The Ten People Test asks a blind judge to match a sentence to one of two readers’ profiles; chance is 50%, and the corpus scores 60.0% (95% CI 52.7 to 66.9). Its first run scored 72.8%, and that was a leak in the test, not a result: every fix since has lowered the number. A Forer test, where people rate their own reading blind against someone else’s, is collecting its first 17 clean ratings.

02 · One for checking my own AI systems

OUROBOROS

Commission the research, audit it against what’s running, then check the decision actually shipped.

OUROBOROS is a research, audit and decision process I ran on my own AI agent system. It began with a commissioned report, about 77 sources, on self-improving multi-agent architectures and how to evaluate them.

I did not adopt the report. I audited it line by line against what my system actually ran, kept what fit, and refused the rest with reasons: monthly infrastructure budgets in the thousands, GPU clusters, safety layers sourced to vendor marketing.

Then I checked what shipped. Of six approved changes, one had become enforced code; five were only prose. Now a decision ships with its mechanism, or it is recorded as not done. The system it grew into works the same way: every change it proposes to itself is tested against a fixed set of tasks first, and one that does worse is never adopted. Still in progress, it is heading toward an agent orchestration system.

03 · One for my own DJ sets

PulseEngine

Software I’m building to turn live DJ music into real-time visuals, lyrics and projection.

PulseEngine is real-time visual software for live DJ sets, built for my own. It listens to the music as it plays, locks to the tempo, and drives visuals on the beat. It generates images with AI in real time, transcribes lyrics from the vocals onto the screen as they are sung, and projection-maps the result onto physical surfaces. Motion reactivity is still in development.

The detail that shaped it: an early build crashed on launch from a bug in its audio analysis, and a quieter one made the tempo estimate meaningless. Both are fixed, and the rule since is to test with real sound in the real room before a set, never after.
