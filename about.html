About

Who is doing this

I learned how to run a team of AI agents in a restaurant kitchen.

While my friends watched cartoons, I watched Anthony Bourdain and Gordon Ramsay. I was cooking at home by ten, and I wanted my own restaurant. I was deliberate about it. I studied Business Administration and Economics at Istanbul Bilgi University first, because I knew cooking alone would not be enough, then trained as a chef at MSA, the Culinary Arts Academy in Istanbul. Then kitchens.

A kitchen runs on fourteen to seventeen hour days, and one small mistake can undo a whole service. It holds together because of the brigade: every station owns its section, and no plate leaves until it crosses the pass and someone checks it.

But the hours were taking more from my life than the dream was worth, so I let it go. I tried marketing, sales, consulting and a few ventures of my own, and each one failed for a different reason. So I moved to Toronto and started Business Insights and Analytics at Humber College, to sharpen the tools I would need.

Building on my own, I was doing every job in my company myself. So I built a French brigade of AI agents in Claude Code. Then it broke the way a new kitchen breaks. I was running 15 sessions at once on a MacBook. My hand-kept list of what each one was doing was wrong on 15 of 22 rows. The first night I left the agents alone produced four coordination failures, and every one of them was a problem a kitchen brigade solves by design.

So I ran it like a kitchen. Each agent owns one station. A separate agent checks the work before it reaches me. One board shows what is alive and what is waiting. Every rule logs each time it fires, and a rule that keeps firing wrongly gets demoted. When a number looks too good, I look for the leak. One of my tests scored 72.8% until I closed the leaks in it. Every fix since has lowered the number, and the lowest one is the one I publish.

That is what I bring to you, in two halves. The cook and the analyst cost your menu from your own invoices and tell you plainly what to reprice, feature, rework or drop. I have done it for one paying client. The brigade builder sets up Claude Code for the jobs you repeat, and tests it on a clean machine before you get it.

I still work like a cook: nothing leaves my kitchen until someone has checked it. Off the clock, I DJ, and I build the tools I needed and could not find.
